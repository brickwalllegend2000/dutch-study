# Giving instructions — Chapter 3 practice

Practice for Chapter 3 grammar. Canonical note: [Modal verbs: can, must, may and want](../wiki/grammar/modal-verbs-can-must-may-and-want.md).

Learn the difference between **moeten**, **mogen** and **kunnen**, then practise giving four clear instructions.

## Choose the right meaning

| Dutch | English meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Je moet … + infinitive | You must / have to … | Je moet een afspraak maken. — You have to make an appointment. |
| Je mag … + infinitive | You may / are allowed to … | Je mag hier picknicken. — You may picnic here. |
| Je mag niet … + infinitive | You must not / are not allowed to … | Je mag hier niet parkeren. — You must not park here. |
| Je hoeft niet … + te + infinitive | You do not have to … | Je hoeft niet te bellen. — You do not have to call. |
| Je kan / kunt … + infinitive | You can …; ability or possibility | Je kan online een afspraak maken. — You can make an appointment online. |
| Je wil / wilt … + infinitive | You want to … | Je wil de school bezoeken. — You want to visit the school. |

**Do not confuse:** “Je mag niet komen” means you are not allowed to come. “Je hoeft niet te komen” means coming is not necessary. Matches the wiki distinction between prohibition (*niet mogen*) and lack of necessity.

### Word order

The conjugated modal verb is in the second position; the infinitive goes at the end. See [modal verbs](../wiki/grammar/modal-verbs-can-must-may-and-want.md).

**Je moet het formulier invullen.** — You have to complete the form.

**Eerst moet je het formulier invullen.** — First you have to complete the form.

“Eerst” occupies the first position, so **moet** comes before **je**.

| Subject | moeten | mogen | kunnen | willen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ik | moet | mag | kan | wil |
| jij / je | moet | mag | kan / kunt | wil / wilt |
| u | moet | mag | kan / kunt | wil / wilt |
| hij / zij / het | moet | mag | kan | wil |
| wij / jullie / zij (plural) | moeten | mogen | kunnen | willen |

Forms match the [wiki table](../wiki/grammar/modal-verbs-can-must-may-and-want.md).

## Direct and polite instructions

The **imperative** is a direct instruction, normally without a subject. For common verbs, use the ik-form: maken → maak; lezen → lees; schrijven → schrijf. Learn irregular forms such as **Wees rustig** — Be calm.

| Infinitive | Instruction | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lezen | Lees de tekst. | Read the text. |
| schrijven | Schrijf je naam. | Write your name. |
| maken | Maak een afspraak. | Make an appointment. |
| bellen | Bel naar de school. | Call the school. |
| invullen | Vul het formulier in. | Complete the form. |
| opendoen | Doe het raam open. | Open the window. |
| meenemen | Neem je identiteitskaart mee. | Bring your identity card. |
| wachten | Wacht even. | Wait a moment. |

With separable verbs, the particle goes later in the clause: **Vul je naam in.** With a modal, keep the infinitive together: **Je moet je naam invullen.**

For a polite request: **Kan je het raam sluiten, alstublieft?** — Can you close the window, please? More formal: **Kunt u het raam sluiten, alstublieft?**

For a negative instruction: **Parkeer hier niet.** — Do not park here. **Maak geen lawaai.** — Do not make noise.

The “subject in every sentence” writing rule has an exception here: a normal imperative omits the subject.

### Model: registering for a course

Eerst moet je de website openen. Daarna moet je het formulier invullen. Je moet je naam en je e-mailadres schrijven. Controleer de informatie. Ten slotte moet je het formulier versturen.

**Meaning:** First open the website. Then complete the form, enter your name and email address, check the information and send the form.

### Model: classroom rules

Je moet op tijd komen. Je mag vragen stellen. Je mag niet eten in de klas. Je moet de ramen sluiten voor je vertrekt.

**Meaning:** Arrive on time; questions are allowed; eating in class is not allowed; close the windows before leaving.

### B. Give four pieces of advice

Write four sentences answering “Ik heb ruzie met mijn vriend. Wat moet ik doen?” — “I have had an argument with my boyfriend/friend. What should I do?”

**Model answer**

Je kan rustig met hem praten. Je moet goed naar hem luisteren. Je mag hem niet uitschelden. Daarna kunnen jullie samen een oplossing zoeken.

You can talk calmly with him. You should listen carefully to him. You must not call him names. Afterwards, you can look for a solution together.

### Writing check

- Does each instruction match the intended meaning: obligation, permission or prohibition?
- Is the modal conjugated and the infinitive at the end?
- Is a separable verb split correctly in a direct instruction?
- Did I use a capital letter and punctuation?
- Did I answer every part of the task?

**Two-minute practice:** write three classroom rules using “Je moet”, “Je mag” and “Je mag niet”.
