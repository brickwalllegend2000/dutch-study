*Zal / zullen*

[Grammar guide](../../sources/pages/401f89e5e0fc819da383d5825749b371.md)

Use **zal/zullen + infinitive** for a future action or promise. The message chapter uses it to say what you will do next.

## Examples from the archive

- **Dat zal ik doen!** — I’ll do that!
- **Ik zal ook vragen welke oefeningen ik moet maken.** — I’ll also ask which exercises I need to do.
- **Ik zal wat later zijn.** — I’ll be a little late. The archive supplies this opening before want/omdat.

## Pattern

**Subject + zal/zullen + other information + infinitive.**

- **ik zal; hij/zij zal**
- **jij/je zal or zult; u zal or zult**
- **wij/we, jullie, zij/ze zullen**

In **Dat zal ik doen**, **dat** comes first, so the conjugated verb **zal** comes before the subject **ik**. **Doen** is the infinitive at the end.

## Try it

1. Ik … wat later … . (zal + zijn)
2. Start with **Dat**: Ik zal dat doen.
3. Change **Ik zal dat doen** to **Wij …**.

Answers

1. Ik **zal** wat later **zijn**.
2. **Dat zal ik doen.**
3. **Wij zullen dat doen.**

## Review note

Reviewed by an AI agent (Claude) against the course snapshots and general standard-Dutch knowledge. This is not a teacher or native-speaker review. Reviewed 2026-10-23.

- Rule checked: *zal/zullen* plus an infinitive; *zal* with *ik*, *hij* and *zij*; *zullen* in the plural; *zal* or *zult* after *jij*, *je* and *u*. Correct. In a question after *je* the *-t* form is dropped (*zul je*), which the course does not show.
- Examples: *Dat zal ik doen!*, *Ik zal ook vragen …* and *Ik zal wat later zijn* are from the archive (chapter 6). The exercise and answers are added study aids.
- Limit: only the future-promise use is covered. Regional note: in Flanders *gaan* is the more common way to talk about the future in speech; see [Future plans](future-plans-gaan-infinitive.md).
